Campaign map

London to Berlin through the Channel and Paris

PrologueLondonFind a compact car and cross the first bridge.

A delivery yard teaches reversing, steering and collision damage before an evacuation queue collapses at the bridge.

Route choice. Take the open bus lane and risk repeated impacts, or abandon momentum to open the service gate on foot.

Payoff. A radio contact gives Alex the first safehouse location. The car's fuel and damage carry forward.

Act ISouthern EnglandGet tools, fuel and a route to Dover.

A wet motorway pileup forces a choice between a narrow service road and a supply stop with a safer exit but a longer on-foot approach.

New options. Van and pickup capacity, a repair kit and roadside refueling. Samira's workshop teaches how weight changes handling.

Payoff. Open the route to the harbor with a usable vehicle and a recovery option if fuel runs low.

Act I finaleDover & the ChannelRestore the ramp and load the vehicle onto the ferry.

Scout the port, restore loading power and return to the queue before the final embarkation window closes.

Optional rescue. Spend time guiding a stranded group to the ramp. Boarding space and the time remaining are visible before committing.

Payoff. The ferry transition keeps the registered vehicle, secured cargo and completed rescue state.

Act IINorthern FranceReach Paris via farmland or an industrial corridor.

Muddy field tracks suit a buggy or 4×4. Paved factory roads have tighter gates and more salvage.

New options. Trade for off-road tires or spend fuel to reach a supply depot. A tractor can clear a blocked farm route at the cost of speed.

Payoff. Meet a survivor group and carry its help or the supplies you saved into Paris.

Act IIIParisRecover an evacuation relay and reach the eastern exit.

Enter a parking garage on foot and recover the relay. Starting the generator draws infected toward the route back to the car.

Route choice. The boulevard offers speed through a crowd. The flooded underpass trades crowd density for poor grip and a blocked lane. A separate Seine encounter tests boat handling and boarding threats.

Payoff. Rescuing the garage mechanic earns a repair discount at the next hub.

Act IVEastbound borderlandsCross forests, freight yards and industrial barriers.

Heavy weather cuts visibility around a freight junction. A bulldozer can open the main lane, while a small vehicle can reach an alternate access road.

Tradeoff. Heavy machinery clears the obstacle but draws a larger response. The bypass preserves fuel and exposes the player during a manual gate sequence.

Payoff. The border checkpoint registers vehicle damage and the route information recovered from the yard.

Act VBerlinReach the evacuation network as rival groups close in.

Jonas offers a freight-yard rendezvous. Access and assistance depend on earlier rescues, alliances and the supplies carried into the city.

Final decision. Use remaining cargo space for survivors or supplies. A limited armored vehicle or patrol boat can support specific branches.

Payoff. The finale resolves who reaches the rendezvous and which resources remain for evacuation.

Late campaignAircraft extractionMission-specific helicopter and small-plane sequences.

The helicopter uses a marked landing area. The small plane requires a prepared runway. Each has a dedicated approach, exit and recovery checkpoint.

Design gate. Prototype camera comfort, landing readability and recoverable missed approaches before adding either sequence to the campaign.

Vertical slice

Proposed 10–15 minute London-to-outskirts mission

Out of London

Alex leaves a neighborhood safehouse in a battered sedan. Reach the motorway checkpoint with a running engine and enough fuel to continue. The route takes the player through a vulnerable fuel stop, a foot-to-car escape and a choice that changes the condition of the vehicle at the finish.

  • One sedan
  • Walkers and a screamer
  • Two escape routes
  • Checkpoint and reload

See the production brief

  1. Leave the safehouse

    Teach braking, reverse and the chase camera on a short road. Introduce the low-fuel cue before the junction.

  2. Approach the fuel stop

    Read the blocked road from the driver’s seat. Park at a valid exit point, then cross the forecourt on foot.

  3. Refuel under pressure

    Let the player interrupt the fill and keep fuel already transferred. A visual cue and distinct audio tell signal the screamer before it calls.

  4. Choose the escape route

    The high street is shorter and crowded. The service lane is longer and has fewer infected, with a narrow turn and poor grip.

  5. Reach the checkpoint

    Record route, fuel, engine condition and damage. Explain why the run succeeded or failed, and let the player retry from a consistent snapshot.

Playtest acceptance

  • At least 16 of 20 first-time testers complete the slice unaided. Log stalls, deaths, refueling choices and fuel remaining.
  • Both escape routes succeed from the intended checkpoint state. Test blocked doors, canceled refuels, empty fuel and interrupted saves for recovery.
  • Test the full sequence with keyboard and controller, remapped inputs, reduced motion and visual sound cues enabled.

Production gates

  • One near-final sedan with readable damage, a polished fuel stop and two distinct infected silhouettes with complete interaction feedback.
  • Profile rain, crowd, collisions and streaming together on agreed hardware. The provisional performance-mode target is 60 fps, or about 16.7 ms per frame.
  • Lock the region size, hardware targets and content pipeline after the slice. Prototype boats and aircraft after the core road loop passes.

AAA production brief

Visual direction, playtest criteria and funding decisions for Out of London

The forecourt in the rain

Alex coasts onto a dark service-station forecourt with the fuel warning on. A generator keeps one patch of canopy light alive. The car ticks as it cools. While fuel transfers, a knock against a shutter competes with the rain. The player decides whether another few seconds of fuel are worth giving up a clear exit.

Carry the consequences into the getaway. A bent wheel pulls on the steering, a patch of standing water reduces grip, and a quiet service lane offers more room to recover than the crowded high street. At the checkpoint, Alex can hear what happened back at the station over the radio.

Art direction and authenticityEnvironment, lighting, vehicle condition and local character

Stage the same scene at three distances

From the approach, the canopy, safe parking space and two road exits should read in the headlight beam. On foot, sell the location through faded lane paint, cables, rain channels, stock cages and handwritten notices. Up close, show hand contact at the fuel cap, wet fabric on sleeves and grime around the sedan's moving parts.

Build a recognizable place

Use a fictional south London district as the reference. Check street widths, signs, road markings, building uses, interiors and language against local references. Apply the same review in Dover, Paris and Berlin. Keep the art palette grounded in each location and grade every region from the same exposure reference.

Make surface behavior agree with the picture

Reserve sharp reflections for puddles and glazed surfaces. Keep wet asphalt rough enough for lane markings to read. Road texture, tire sound and vehicle response should agree when the car moves from tarmac onto standing water or loose gravel.

Inspect through the gameplay camera

Review the sedan intact and damaged from the chase camera, from the door during entry and under headlights in rain. Verify light placement, glass, wheel clearance, panel seams and contact. Capture the same camera positions for every art review.

Animation, sound and player feedbackWhat the player sees, hears and controls during a transition

Car to on foot

Brake to a valid stop, show the available door, plant the character's feet and blend to the on-foot camera. Test both sides of the car and a sloped parking position. Interrupted entry leaves Alex in a navigable position and restores the correct controls.

Fuel transfer under pressure

Give fueling distinct start, transfer and stop feedback. The gauge and interaction sound update together. Canceling keeps the transferred fuel and immediately restores movement. During the screamer's wind-up, a directional icon accompanies the sound and remains readable with subtitles enabled.

Damage and handling cues

Use one cue each for a damaged wheel, overheating and critical engine condition. Pair the visible effect with a HUD icon, text and a distinct sound. Keep controls predictable until the condition changes and present a recovery option where the route allows one.

Layer the soundscape

Set the rain, canopy drip and engine idle levels in an empty forecourt. Add cab occlusion and distance to the door, fuel transfer and infected cues. Mix radio dialogue against engine load and music so the urgent cue remains intelligible during the getaway.

Narrative consequencesOne optional rescue, visible outcomes and a controlled scope

Put character in the choice

Alex hears someone call from the shuttered kiosk. The station worker knows how to open a shortcut off the service lane. Stopping to help costs time while the player is low on fuel. Both original routes remain available.

Carry state into the payoff

If rescued, the worker opens the shortcut and later radios a useful report about the motorway. If Alex leaves, the debrief reflects that choice. At the checkpoint, show route taken, fuel remaining and help received.

Build the critical path first

A single radio guide and one optional interaction are enough to test whether players care about the choice. A larger companion system and fully staged cinematics depend on the result of that test.

Verify both outcomes

Run the mission with the worker rescued and with the rescue skipped. Load the checkpoint immediately after the choice and confirm radio, route access, reward and debrief agree.

Performance and quality verificationRepeatable captures and acceptance criteria

Agree on the test machine

Select a baseline PC and record its hardware, the build ID, resolution and graphics settings. Use the same route and capture points. Console targets depend on hardware access and native profiling.

Capture the worst section

In a 60 fps performance mode, start from a proposed 16.7 ms frame budget. Record frame-time percentiles, CPU and GPU timings, peak memory and the longest streaming hitch during the rain-and-crowd getaway. Profile three comparable runs from a clean launch and retain a capture for each route.

Test recovery before polish sign-off

Repeat the route with partial fuel, a damaged wheel and a blocked door. Interrupt fueling and checkpoint creation. Verify that inventory, damage, route access and prompts match the last valid snapshot after relaunch.

Measure clarity with new players

Use the slice target of 16 successful unaided completions in a 20-player cohort. Ask what prompted each player to stop fueling, which route they picked, and why their run ended. Record route choices and separate first-run findings from experienced-player tuning.

Production gates

Required evidence before committing to the next build

  1. Playable blockout

    One sedan, one short route and one infected. Demonstrate driving, entry and exit, an interruptible refuel and checkpoint restart with temporary assets.

    Exit criterion. A captured run and notes from five first-time players show where the core loop succeeds or breaks.

  2. Art and systems benchmark

    Finish a small forecourt, one hero car and two infected silhouettes. Review the same scene from the chase camera, at the pump and during the escape.

    Exit criterion. Source and asset rights are recorded, replay captures include lighting, sound and animation, and performance has a measured baseline.

  3. Vertical slice sign-off

    Complete both escape routes with final-intent feedback, a readable optional choice and save recovery. Gather test results on the agreed machine.

    Exit criterion. The cohort target, recovery checks and frame-time budget are met or have a documented fix before committing further.

  4. Campaign production decision

    Use the slice to measure person-days per finished road segment, building kit, vehicle variant, encounter and voiced scene. Estimate team needs and budget from that throughput.

    Exit criterion. Review one funded campaign scope, a reduced scope and the cost of supporting each target platform. Commit city count and release scope after that review.

Out of London — Vertical Slice SpecificationMission beats, routes, encounter rules and build scope

View Markdown file on GitHub ↗

Status · Proposed for pre-production review
Target · One complete single-player PC mission in 10–15 minutes
Player · Alex Morgan, driving a battered sedan from a neighborhood safehouse to a motorway checkpoint

Mission contract

The player leaves with enough fuel to reach the station and must refuel to have a reliable margin for the escape. The station is the moment where the driving and on-foot loops meet. The player can watch the gauge rise while the forecourt becomes less safe, then decide when to stop fueling and which route to take. The slice ends with a running car reaching the motorway checkpoint. Its debrief explains the route taken, fuel left, vehicle condition and optional rescue outcome.

Required for the slice Deferred past the slice
Sedan, chase camera, one drive-to-foot-to-drive sequence, interruptible fuel transfer, readable wheel damage and recovery Additional vehicles, garage progression, water and aircraft, companion AI, systemic city simulation
One safehouse, one forecourt, two escape routes, two infected archetypes, one optional rescue, two resumable checkpoints Campaign branching, voiced cinematics, dynamic weather cycles, console certification

Route and pacing

  1. Neighborhood safehouse
  2. Approach and park
  3. Fuel stop under pressure

Choose the high street or the service lane. Both routes end at the motorway checkpoint.

Beat Pacing target Player action and success cue
Leave the safehouse 1–2 min Steer, brake and reverse before reaching traffic. Following the route sign leads to a low-fuel cue and the station approach.
Approach the forecourt 2–3 min Spot the blocked junction and a place to park. Exit from a valid door and orient toward the fuel point.
Refuel under pressure 3–4 min Start the fill, see fuel accumulate and hear the screamer's warning. Canceling preserves transferred fuel. The kiosk rescue can be attempted here.
Escape 3–4 min Re-enter the car and choose the shorter crowded high street or the longer service lane with a narrow turn and low grip. The rescue opens a shortcut off the service lane.
Reach the checkpoint 1–2 min Cross the marked motorway threshold under the car's own power. Show the route, condition, fuel and rescue result, then permit replay.

Pacing estimates include orientation and first-time reading. Tune the overall run to the stated target before locking individual beats.

Encounter behavior

  • Approach. Let the player see the canopy, parking opportunity and both road exits before the danger escalates. A blocked driver-side door offers a reachable alternate entry or a clear blocked cue.
  • Pressure. Start with walkers at a distance. The screamer telegraphs escalation with sound and a directional visual cue. If its call completes, the authored encounter budget admits reinforcements through occluded entrances. Give the player time to disengage and preserve a readable route back to the car.
  • Optional rescue. Alex can help a worker behind the kiosk shutter. Success unlocks the service-lane shortcut and a radio report. Skipping the rescue preserves both original routes. Set the rescue flag only after the interaction resolves and keep it consistent on reload.
  • Route balance. The high street trades short distance for crowd pressure and collision risk. The service lane trades lower enemy density for restricted steering clearance and poor grip. Both routes must be reachable and completable with the intended unmodified sedan and a partial fill established by playtests.

Progress, failure and recovery

State Trigger Recovery or persistence
Start checkpoint Leave the safehouse Restore car, fuel, damage, player and encounter state together.
Forecourt checkpoint Cross the forecourt approach threshold before any fuel or rescue change Restore approach state, preserve the same valid parking options and reset encounter progress.
Cancel fuel transfer Release or cancel the fueling interaction Return control promptly and preserve the completed amount exactly once.
Out of fuel Tank reaches zero Offer an authored recovery canister where reachable. Otherwise show a clear retry and return to the forecourt checkpoint.
Car unusable or player down Engine cannot drive or the player is defeated Show the reason, then allow retry. Retrying restores the selected checkpoint and removes temporary encounter state.
Mission complete Reach motorway threshold with running engine Lock a single completion event and summarize route, fuel, damage and rescue.

Minimum build list

Owner role Deliverable
Level design One connected route with two exits, a safehouse apron, forecourt, kiosk, alternative door clearances and recovery points
Gameplay Sedan handling, camera, foot controls, interaction ownership, fuel transfer, wheel damage, threat state, rescue flag and checkpoint logic
Art and audio One hero sedan, forecourt kit, walker and screamer silhouettes, entry and exit animations, rain, headlights, audio cues and simple radio dialogue
UI and QA Remappable inputs, fuel and condition feedback, directional threat cues, debrief, route and checkpoint tests

Definition of done

  • A packaged build is completable through either base route. The rescue outcome and shortcut survive a checkpoint replay without precluding the main objective.
  • At least 16 of 20 first-time participants complete the mission unaided on the agreed build. Record where they stop, why they cancel fueling, which route they choose and whether they can explain the result.
  • No progression blocker remains across the save, transition, fuel and accessibility checks in the Playtest and QA Plan.
  • The wet-weather escape has repeatable performance captures on the agreed PC against the provisional 60 fps mode. Hardware and tuning values require approval before they become fixed targets.

Technical Design DocumentEngine evaluation, system ownership, checkpoints and profiling

Status · Proposed architecture for the Out of London vertical slice
Engine decision · Unreal Engine is the current candidate for the AAA slice. Record the selection after a repeatable handling, camera, crowd and packaging benchmark on the available PC.

Platform and benchmarking contract

The initial target is a packaged Windows PC build with keyboard, mouse and controller support. Record CPU, GPU, RAM, driver, resolution, graphics preset and build revision before profiling. Use a proposed 60 fps performance mode, which gives 16.7 ms per frame. Collect CPU and GPU frame times, p95 and p99 frame times, peak memory and the largest streaming hitch through the same rainy escape. Establish stricter CPU, GPU, memory and hitch budgets from the first measured baseline. Platform expansion depends on profiling and available hardware.

Runtime ownership

System Owns Contract at the boundary
Mission director Mission phase, objective, route, rescue flag, checkpoint eligibility, end reason Consumes domain events once and reports state to UI and checkpoint services.
Player and vehicle controllers Input mode, current occupant, valid door, camera target, health and vehicle movement A transition transfers control only when the other mode can accept it. If interrupted, restore a valid previous mode and position.
Vehicle condition and resources Fuel, component condition, usable movement and active transfer Publish elapsed fuel transfer in bounded increments. Cancel, interruption or checkpoint restore must never double-count a transfer.
Encounter director Infected budget, legal spawn areas, cues, pursuit and cleanup Reacts to mission phase and noise. Spawn candidates must be occluded and reachable, with a budget and a valid escape route.
Level and streaming Cells, collision, navigation and authored spawn or recovery anchors Preload approaching junction cells using predicted travel speed. Keep previous safe space available through transitions.
Checkpoint service Versioned, validated snapshot and last known-good backup Commit a complete generation before promoting it. Restore all authoritative game state before enabling controls or encounters.
Presentation HUD, audio, haptics, animation, subtitles and accessibility settings Derive from authoritative state. A HUD gauge, door prompt or animation cue cannot complete a gameplay action on its own.

Proposed data contracts

  • VehicleTune defines mass, steering and braking curves, fuel consumption, grip response and component thresholds. Author paved, wet and gravel surface responses as editable data.
  • RouteTune defines segments, checkpoint anchors, recovery positions and encounter caps. Store route identifiers independently of level object names so checkpoints survive reasonable level revisions.
  • EncounterTune defines legal entry volumes, warning duration, awareness ranges, active budgets and cleanup. Use full movement and collision for near threats and cheaper behavior for distant shapes. Measure the transition distance in a playable build.
  • CheckpointSnapshot stores schema version, checkpoint ID, mission flags, route, player state, vehicle position and condition, fuel, inventory and checkpoint generation. Reconstruct active encounter state from a valid mission phase instead of serializing temporary visual effects.

Failure handling

Case Required behavior
Entry door becomes obstructed mid-transition Cancel cleanly, clear the interaction lock and place the player at a navigable point with the prior input mode.
Player stops fueling at the same instant as a hit or pause Commit the transferred amount once and close the interaction. The gauge and snapshot agree.
A segment fails to stream or navigation is missing Hold at a readable boundary or use an alternate safe anchor. Log the cell, phase and route so QA can reproduce it.
A later encounter collides with a prior trigger Ignore duplicate mission or completion events. Bound the active enemy budget.
Latest save write fails or data is invalid on load Recover from the last complete validated checkpoint and surface the recovery result to the player.

Implementation order

  1. Ship a packaged greybox with the sedan, chase camera and one repeatable road loop.
  2. Add on-foot entry and exit, fuel transfer, reusable checkpoint state and valid recovery positions.
  3. Add the encounter director, two authored routes and the optional rescue. Test the encounter with temporary assets.
  4. Integrate near-final visual, animation and audio assets. Profile the busiest segment with rain, streaming and collisions enabled.

Open decisions · Record the exact engine version, baseline PC, job ownership and telemetry policy at the first milestone. Reproduce tests locally before electing to collect any player telemetry.

Visual target

The forecourt sits in a fictional south London district. Use observed street proportions, road markings, kerb wear, service-station signage and everyday props to establish place. The canopy is a navigation landmark. Headlights reveal a candidate parking spot and the route back to the sedan. During the escape, protect the silhouettes of infected, drivable gaps and the road edge even when rain fills the frame.

Use playable chase and on-foot views for every acceptance capture. Lock the exposure reference and capture an intact sedan, a damaged sedan, the camera transition and the escape. Repeat the same views as models, effects and lighting change.

Asset workflow

  1. Record a reference, named owner, source provenance and intended camera distance for every hero asset.
  2. Block out scale, pivot, movement, contact and collision with temporary material. Review under playable lighting before finishing detail.
  3. Deliver editable source plus runtime files, material assignments, textures, quality tiers and an in-engine test capture. Use measured scene budgets when choosing texture size, draw calls and levels of detail.
  4. Accept an asset after visual, gameplay and performance checks on the agreed build. Track the build and asset revision together so later regressions can be reproduced.

Rights register

Keep one row per externally sourced asset, including textures, fonts, audio, motion data and scans. Retain the source URL, creator, acquisition date, license evidence, modification and redistribution terms, credit requirement, intended use, and reviewer. A placeholder remains marked provisional until rights are recorded. Capture any credits with the asset revision that ships.

Status · Proposed sign-off plan for the Out of London vertical slice
Release candidate · Freeze the exact build, settings and test machine before evaluating the cohort. Record fixes and retest the affected cases on a new build.

Gates

Gate Method Pass criterion
Greybox comprehension Five first-time players run the temporary-art build. Observe the controls, valid door, fuel transfer and escape cue. Document the observed failure points. Resolve any repeated loss of control or progression blocker before expanding the build.
Slice completion Twenty first-time players start independently using the same candidate build. External coaching counts as assisted completion. At least 16 of 20 finish unaided. Report assisted completions, skips, retries and timing separately.
Correctness Run the route, transition and checkpoint matrix on keyboard and controller. Repeat every blocker after fixes. Both base routes complete, no reproducible progression blocker remains, and checkpoint state matches the last valid save in all recovery cases.
Performance Profile the rain-and-crowd escape from a clean launch on the agreed PC. Capture three comparable runs spanning both routes. Assess the provisional 60 fps budget, p95 and p99 frame times, peak memory and worst hitch. Record the measured thresholds and approval before sign-off.

Scenario matrix

ID Test Expected result
QA-01 Complete each base route without the kiosk rescue on the intended partial fill. Both runs reach the motorway. Debrief shows the correct route, fuel and vehicle condition.
QA-02 Complete the rescue, then use the shortcut and reach the checkpoint. Shortcut, radio report and debrief agree. Retry resolves the prior checkpoint's rescue state consistently.
QA-03 Park with a door blocked on a slope. Interrupt re-entry while under pressure. The alternate door or blocked cue is legible. Input and camera return to a valid mode.
QA-04 Cancel fueling at several progress points. Repeat during an enemy hit and a pause. The transferred volume is credited exactly once. HUD, next interaction and saved state agree.
QA-05 Run the tank empty and damage the car past the drivable threshold. Recover fuel from the authored location if available. Otherwise show a clear failure reason and reachable retry.
QA-06 Interrupt a new checkpoint write and relaunch. Repeat with an invalid latest snapshot in a QA build. Last complete checkpoint loads with corresponding fuel, damage, position, inventory and flags.
QA-07 Approach, leave and re-enter a streaming boundary during the busiest chase. Navigable surfaces and collision stay consistent. The game keeps a valid exit or recovery anchor.
QA-08 Play with audio muted, alternative inputs, subtitles, reduced motion and visual sound cues. Fuel warnings, threat direction, door state, pause, resume and restart are usable on both input types.
QA-09 Replay the final checkpoint and attempt to cross the finish threshold twice. Completion fires once. Restart starts the intended checkpoint with repeatable mission state.

Session and reporting protocol

  • Record anonymized session ID, build, test machine, input mode, configuration, first run completion, route, rescue choice, retries and assist points. Capture timing for fuel transfer start, departure and finish. Record only consented footage or notes.
  • Observe without directing the player. Ask afterward how they knew they could cancel fueling, why they picked their route and what caused a failure or success.
  • For defects, retain build, severity, fixture or checkpoint, steps, expected and actual behavior, and evidence. A blocker prevents finish or recovery. A major defect obscures a critical cue or makes a supported route unreliable. Track other defects for the next triage.
  • Sign-off records include the QA matrix, anonymized cohort results, profiling captures and the remaining known issues with owners. Apply a documented exit decision to every unmet target.
